How a decision becomes behavior

Observe→Retrieve memory→Choose one tool→Validate→Execute→Verify and remember

Python owns the loop and completion checks. The director plans; the actor acts. Handwritten controllers handle selected chores. C# reads structured game state and executes bounded controls. State and visual are modes of one actor; Python selects between them. They share objectives and memories. A usual action needs one actor call; inspect_scene adds a visual call next, while director reviews run separately.

World time continues during thinking.

A still farmer can mean inference latency. Routine director reviews can run concurrently with actor work, but new-goal planning and actor decisions wait for their model responses.

    Decisions worth reviewing
    How much freedom should the farmer have?
    The farmer may now interrupt work, follow surprises and change its objective without director approval. It chooses how to weigh its interests against unfinished responsibilities. Observe how well that balance works in live play.

    What should count as an experience?
    The actor reports a subjective reaction after an attempted control. The journal retains outcome, conditions and compact evidence, but subject identity and interpretation are still model-supplied. The farmer now chooses preferences independently, including reactions to uncertain or unavailable interactions. No confidence threshold or encounter count is prescribed.

    Where should automation stop?
    The saved design keeps skills for boring, repeatedly unreliable chores and leaves interesting activities improvised. Better observations and verified outcomes can support improvisation without scripting relationships or exploration.

    What needs to be reliable before an audience watches?
    Return routes and saves, truthful work completion, bounded transport waits, calendar continuity and readable narration during recovery. The full review distinguishes demonstrated failures from newly identified code risks.
